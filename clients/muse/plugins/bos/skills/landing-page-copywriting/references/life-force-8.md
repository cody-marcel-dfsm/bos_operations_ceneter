# Life-Force 8 Reference

Drew Eric Whitman's *CA$HVERTISING* presents these eight marketing appeals. Treat them as a copywriting framework, not a complete scientific model of motivation. Select desires from real audience evidence and validate important choices through customer research or testing.

| Desire | Customer meaning | Useful page language and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension | Feel healthy, capable, energetic, and able to enjoy life | Show the better daily life, health or safety outcome, expert support, and credible results. |
| Enjoyment of food and beverages | Enjoy taste, freshness, satisfaction, and shared meals | Use honest sensory words, appetizing visuals, ingredients, reviews, and ease of preparation. |
| Freedom from fear, pain, and danger | Feel safe and remove a known threat, worry, or source of pain | Name the real concern calmly; add guarantees, safety facts, credentials, privacy, and clear expectations. |
| Sexual companionship | Feel attractive, confident, close, and connected | Focus on confidence, intimacy, connection, and respect. Avoid objectification or unsupported promises. |
| Comfortable living conditions | Gain ease, rest, convenience, time, and a pleasant environment | Show fewer steps, less work, time saved, comfort, simple setup, and dependable support. |
| Superiority, winning, keeping up with others | Achieve more, improve skill, gain status, or avoid falling behind | Use measurable progress, mastery, premium quality, comparison, or recognition without belittling others. |
| Care and protection of loved ones | Help family, children, partners, employees, friends, or pets thrive | Show responsible care, protection, future benefit, and evidence relevant to the loved one. |
| Social approval | Belong, feel accepted, gain recognition, and choose what trusted peers choose | Use genuine reviews, customer counts, community, referrals, and relatable customer stories. |

## Selection method

1. Complete: `They want this because they deeply want to ______.`
2. Match that answer to one primary desire.
3. Choose supporting desires only when they strengthen the same decision.
4. Express the desire as a customer outcome rather than naming the framework.
5. Pair the emotional outcome with proof and a clear next step.

Avoid forcing all eight desires into one page. A focused appeal is easier to understand and test.
